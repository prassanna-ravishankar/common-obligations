obligationsIntro:

objections:
  heading: The strongest objections
  items:
    - claim: This would hand more power to the companies it is meant to check.
      answer: It could, if assessments are expensive, proprietary and shaped by incumbents. So the methods must be open to examination, small builders need support with testing costs, requirements must follow what a system can do rather than who built it, and the institutions themselves need published funding, conflict disclosures and an appeal route.
    - claim: Nobody can enforce this across borders.
      answer: Not completely, and not at first. The honest goal is to make compliance inspectable and consequential among those who take part, then widen participation. A public record for each system can exist before any treaty, and buyers can require it today.
    - claim: The companies already publish safety commitments.
      answer: An announcement is not an arrangement in operation. These obligations say who must answer a finding, what evidence supports a claim, and what happens when a commitment fails.
ask:
  heading: What it asks
  lede: The proposal starts small and practical. It can begin before any international agreement exists.
  groups:
    - who: Builders and operators
      items:
        - Publish a record for each system and version, stating which obligations apply, what evidence supports them, who examined it, what remains unresolved, and when it will be reassessed.
        - Enforce limits outside the model and report serious failures through a shared process.
    - who: Buyers and public bodies
      items:
        - Require that record before buying, and write review access and the consequences of misleading claims into contracts.
        - Give the people affected by a purchased system a way to challenge its decisions.
    - who: Governments and regulators
      items:
        - Set enforceable requirements for higher-risk systems, supported by independent technical evaluation, with common minimums agreed between countries.
        - Keep emergency powers time-limited and reviewable, and answer for your own deployments.
